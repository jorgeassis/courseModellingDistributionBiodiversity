### **The Presentation Structure**

Students must structure their presentations to mirror a scientific talk.

Up to 8 minutes presentation.

This should include the following distinct sections:

- **1. The Ecological Problem & Research Question:**  What species or ecosystem are they studying?
	- What is the specific global change threat (e.g., ocean warming, invasive expansion)?
	- *Example:* "How will the distribution of the European Green Crab shift under SSP3-7.0 climate projections?"
- **2. The Modeling Rationale:**  Why is Species Distribution Modeling (SDM) the appropriate tool to answer this question?
	- What are the limitations of the data they are using?
- **3. Hypotheses:**  Based on their knowledge of the species' fundamental niche, what do they expect the model to show? (e.g., "I hypothesize a poleward shift in habitat suitability due to thermal constraints").
- **4. Methodology & Model Explanation:**  What occurrence data and environmental variables (e.g., Bio-ORACLE) were used? Which algorithms were chosen (e.g., Random Forest, or a full Ensemble) and why?
- **5. Results (The Maps & Metrics):**  Visual showcase of their current and projected habitat suitability maps.
	- A brief review of model evaluation metrics (e.g., AUC, TSS) to prove model reliability.
	- A look at variable importance (e.g., which environmental factor drove the distribution?).
	- A look at projected habitat suitability maps
- **6. Discussion & Management Implications:**  Were the hypotheses supported?
	- How can these maps be used in the real world (e.g., Marine Spatial Planning, invasive species management, IUCN Red List assessments)?

| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Ecological Formulation (15 pts)**<br>*Research Question & Hypotheses* | **(13–15 pts)** Question is highly specific and relevant to global change. Hypotheses are explicitly stated and grounded in solid niche theory (e.g., thermal limits, BAM framework). | **(9–12 pts)** Question is clear but slightly broad. Hypotheses are stated but lack deep ecological justification or connection to the specific species' traits. | **(0–8 pts)** Vague or missing research question. Hypotheses are absent, untestable, or ecologically unsound. |
| **2. Methodological Rigor (30 pts)**<br>*Data, Algorithms & Tuning* | **(26–30 pts)** Excellent data curation (e.g., spatial thinning, VIF handled). Choice of algorithm(s) is strongly justified. Mentions parameter tuning, background/pseudo-absence generation, and cross-validation. | **(18–25 pts)** Standard data pipeline used but misses nuanced checks (e.g., didn't address spatial autocorrelation). Algorithm choice is stated but weakly justified. | **(0–17 pts)** Data biases ignored. "Black-box" approach to modeling—algorithms run with default settings without understanding or explanation. |
| **3. Results & Evaluation (20 pts)**<br>*Metrics, Maps & Variable Importance* | **(17–20 pts)** Maps are clear, properly scaled, and easy to read. Model evaluation relies on multiple metrics (e.g., AUC *and* TSS) with an understanding of what they mean. Variable importance is clearly explained. | **(13–16 pts)** Maps are present but lack clarity (e.g., poor color ramps, missing legends). Mentions evaluation metrics but doesn't interpret them well. Variable importance is listed but not contextualized. | **(0–12 pts)** Maps are confusing or missing. No mention of model accuracy/evaluation metrics, or fundamentally misinterprets what the metrics mean. |
| **4. Synthesis & Application (20 pts)**<br>*Discussion & Policy Impact* | **(17–20 pts)** Directly answers the research question and evaluates the hypotheses. Thoughtfully addresses model limitations/uncertainty. Provides concrete, actionable management or policy implications. | **(13–16 pts)** Summarizes results but struggles to connect them back to the original hypotheses. Mentions conservation/management broadly without specific applications. | **(0–12 pts)** Conclusion is abrupt. Fails to discuss limitations (assumes the model is perfect) and offers no real-world application. |
| **5. Science Communication (15 pts)**<br>*Delivery, Visuals & Time* | **(13–15 pts)** Strict adherence to the 5–7 minute time limit. Slides are visual (not text-heavy). Delivery is confident, paced well, and professional. Audio/video is clear. | **(9–12 pts)** Slightly under or over time. Slides are a bit text-heavy or cluttered. Delivery is okay but feels entirely read from a script. | **(0–8 pts)** Significantly over or under time limit. Slides are unreadable walls of text. Poor pacing or major technical recording issues. |
